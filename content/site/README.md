# Learn Python, then algorithms

A free, self-paced course that starts from "I have never written a program"
and builds up to solving classic programming problems. Everything runs in
your browser: there is nothing to install and nothing to sign up for.

It is written for beginners, but it is also for two kinds of experienced
engineer:

- **If you have been leaning on AI to write your code** and have noticed
  the syntax slipping, this is a place to get it back. No autocomplete, no
  assistant: just you, a problem, and the checks.
- **If you have interviews coming up**, the Practice section is a hundred
  problems that end with the classic methods, searching, sorting, merging and
  two pointers, written from a plain-words description. Skip straight to it.

```python
print("Hello. Press Run to see this work.")
```

## The goal

By the end, you should be able to sit down with the 100 problems in the
[Practice](01-learn-python/05-practice) section and solve them without
asking anyone for help. Not quickly, and not without mistakes, but on your
own: reading a problem, working out which tool it needs, writing the code,
and fixing it until the checks pass. Everything before Practice exists to
get you there.

## How the course works

Every page is a short lesson with code you can run and change. Read a little,
press **Run**, change the code, run it again. You learn by watching what
happens, not by memorising.

Each lesson ends with a few short **exercises**. You write your answer in the
box, press **Run tests**, and the page tells you which checks passed. Keep
going until they all do. If you are stuck, open a hint; they get more
specific one at a time, and the full approach and a reference solution unlock
once you have used them all or solved it.

Your progress is saved in this browser, so you can close the tab and come
back. It does not follow you to another computer unless you export it from
**Settings** and import it there.

## The path

Work through the sections in order. Each builds on the one before.

| | Section | What you will be able to do afterwards |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Getting started](01-learn-python/01-getting-started) | Show values, name them, and wrap steps into functions you can reuse. |
| 2 | [Decisions and text](01-learn-python/02-decisions-and-text) | Make a program behave differently depending on its input, and take text apart. |
| 3 | [Collections and loops](01-learn-python/03-collections-and-loops) | Hold many values at once and do something with each of them. |
| 4 | [Stacks, queues and priority queues](01-learn-python/04-stacks-queues-and-priority-queues) | Choose the right structure for "what comes out next". |
| 5 | [Practice](01-learn-python/05-practice) | Reach for the right tool without being told which one. 100 mixed problems. |

Sections 1 to 4 are about 20 pages and take most people a few hours each.
Section 5 is where it sticks: the problems are grouped by difficulty, not by
topic, and the last ten describe classic methods like binary search and
sorting for you to build from the description.

A section on **algorithms** follows and is being written. It will take the
methods from the end of Practice and explain how they work, when to use
them, and how to think about new problems.

## Advice from people who have done this before

- **Forgotten some syntax? Look it up in the lessons, not elsewhere.** The search box at the top of the sidebar finds pages by any word, including names like `popleft` or `sorted`. Going back to the page that taught it rebuilds the memory; a quick answer from somewhere else does not.
- **Type the code yourself.** Copying and pasting a reference solution teaches nothing. If you have to look at the reference, close it and write it from memory.
- **Change the examples.** Every runnable block is an invitation. Break it on purpose and read the error.
- **Do not skip the exercises.** Reading a page feels like understanding. The exercise is where you find out.
- **Stuck for ten minutes? Take a hint.** Stuck for thirty with no hints left? Move on and come back tomorrow. It will be easier.
- **Sit for at least an hour, and give it your full attention.** Close other tabs, silence your phone, and stay with it. Programming needs a stretch of unbroken focus; ten-minute visits never get past the warm-up.
